Where the fifty states balance

The Center of the Nation

Find the Center

A spot on the prairie

Drive north out of Belle Fourche, South Dakota, and the Black Hills fall away behind you into open grassland. About twenty miles up the road, in a cattle pasture in Butte County, lies the geographic center of the United States: 44°58′N, 103°46′W.

That is what this website's name spells out. Four digits of latitude, an N, five digits of longitude, a W. Fifty states, from Maine to Hawaii and Alaska, all balance on this one point.

There is no town here, no gift shop, and usually nobody at all. Just a survey marker, a flag, wind, and grass in every direction. This site tells the story of how a quiet pasture became the middle of America, and how you can go see it.

Reading the Numbers

4458n10346w is a map reference written as a web address. Here is how to decode it.

44°

Degrees north

Forty-four degrees above the equator. That's a little north of halfway between the equator and the North Pole.

58′

Minutes north

Each degree is split into 60 minutes. A minute of latitude is about 1.15 miles, so 58′ puts the point near the top of the 44th degree.

103°

Degrees west

One hundred three degrees west of the Prime Meridian in Greenwich, England, which puts it in the Mountain Time Zone.

46′

Minutes west

At this latitude a minute of longitude is only about 0.8 miles, because meridians draw closer together as you go north.

How the Center Moved

The center of the country is not fixed. It moves when the country changes shape.

1918

The middle of 48 states: Kansas

The U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey found the center of the 48 contiguous states by balancing a cardboard cutout of the map on a pin. It balanced near Lebanon, Kansas, where a monument still stands.

1959

Alaska and Hawaii pull it north and west

When Alaska and Hawaii became states in 1959, the balance point jumped hundreds of miles to the northwest. The Coast and Geodetic Survey placed the new center north of Belle Fourche, South Dakota, at 44°58′N, 103°46′W.

1962

Marking the exact spot

Survey markers were set at the actual site in a private pasture. A flag flies over them today. Because the land is a working ranch, the town of Belle Fourche built its public monument closer to home.

2007

The Center of the Nation Monument

Belle Fourche unveiled a 21-foot granite compass rose, ringed by state flags, outside the Tri-State Museum. It's the place most visitors go to stand at the "center."

In town

Center of the Nation Monument

Belle Fourche, South Dakota

The easy way to visit. The granite compass rose sits behind the Tri-State Museum & Center of the Nation Visitor Center, which tells the story of the center and the surrounding tri-state region.

  • Address: 415 Fifth Avenue, Belle Fourche, SD 57717
  • Getting there: From I-90 exit 10 at Spearfish, about 10 miles north on US 85
  • Monument: Outdoors and open year-round
  • Museum: Free admission; hours vary by season

Museum hours change between summer and winter. Call the visitor center at 605-723-1200 before you make the trip.

On the prairie

The actual center

About 20 miles north of Belle Fourche, Butte County

The true point, 44°58′N 103°46′W, is in a pasture off US 85. A flag marks the spot, and survey markers are set into the ground beneath it.

Getting there means following a gravel ranch road and walking out across the grass. Expect wind, cactus, and cattle. There are no services, so bring water and good shoes.

This is private, working ranch land. Visitors have long been welcome, but please respect the owner's wishes, close any gates behind you, and leave nothing behind.

While You're in the Area

The center sits at the north edge of the Black Hills, one of the best road-trip regions in the country.

Spearfish & Spearfish Canyon

A short drive south of Belle Fourche. The canyon's scenic byway winds past limestone cliffs and waterfalls into the northern Black Hills.

Devils Tower

America's first national monument rises out of the plains just across the Wyoming line, an easy day trip from the center.

Deadwood & Sturgis

The Gold Rush town of Wild Bill Hickok and Calamity Jane, and the home of the famous summer motorcycle rally.

Mount Rushmore

The four presidents are in the heart of the Black Hills, south of Rapid City, about two hours from the center.
